# Manual Post Review

## Scope

This is a qualitative review of the public-visible dataset we collected so far. It uses post text, public/visible engagement, image presence, content type, and platform-relative percentile. It does not use private native analytics such as reach, saves, shares, profile visits, follows, impressions, or audience demographics.

## My Overall Read

Goods Unite Us has a clear content system: short issue/company/politician prompts, links back to the app/site, and a recurring “see how / see where” structure. That consistency is useful, but it also makes many posts feel interchangeable. The strongest posts tend to feel more like a standalone social post: a simple claim, a clear reason to care, and less dependency on the user clicking through immediately.

My perception is that the brand has a strong premise, but the current social execution often jumps too quickly to “download / link / see more.” The posts that land better are the ones that give enough value in the feed before asking the user to act.

## Bluesky Review

| Pattern | What I noticed |
| --- | --- |
| Top image posts | Image posts and short text posts dominate the top visible engagement group. The top Bluesky examples often feel simple, direct, and feed-native rather than database-like. |
| Hashtag-heavy high performers | Several top posts are very short, sometimes mostly hashtags. That suggests some engagement may be driven by network dynamics, timing, image content, or existing audience behavior rather than copy alone. |

| Campaign finance reform posts | “We need campaign finance reform…yesterday” is direct, human, and less templated. This kind of voice may help the account feel less automated. |
| Low-performing card posts | The repeated “See how / See where” posts frequently sit near the bottom on Bluesky. My read is that they feel like app index entries, not posts designed for conversation. |
| Breaking donation alerts | These are useful but not automatically high-performing. Large dollar amounts and recognizable names help, but the format may need a sharper “why this matters” line to become more shareable. |

Bluesky perception: the audience seems more responsive to posts that feel like commentary or a concrete reveal, not just a pointer to a profile page. This does not mean abandoning database-backed content. It means the social wrapper should do more work.

## What I Would Change First

1. Keep the app/database-backed content, but add a feed-native takeaway before the CTA.
2. Use broader app value posts regularly, not only issue/person/company lookup posts.
3. For company/politician cards, test captions that answer “what is surprising here?” before sending users to the app.
4. Reduce CTA clutter where possible. Many posts contain a URL, “link in bio,” app language, and hashtags together.
5. Treat “See how / See where” as a reusable structure, but not the whole post.
6. Use native analytics before making final decisions, because public visible engagement is only a partial performance signal.

## Example Rewrite Directions

These are format directions, not final copy:

| Current pattern | Possible direction |
| --- | --- |
| “See how [Company] feels about [Issue]. Download the free app...” | Lead with the most interesting result or contrast, then mention the app. |
| “See where [Politician] stands on [Issue]...” | Add a one-line summary or timely context before the link. |
| “BREAKING: [Donor] gave $X...” | Add a short “why it matters” line after the factual disclosure. |
| Hashtag-only captions | Keep short captions when the visual is strong, but add one clear sentence for context/searchability. |

## Bottom Line

My perception is that Goods Unite Us already has valuable raw material. The next improvement is not just “post more.” It is to make each post feel less like a database output and more like a complete social object: a fact, a reason to care, and a single next action.
